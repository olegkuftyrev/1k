# ORDINA

## Fire Suppression System Discharge & Cleanup Policy

### Summary

The discharge of a fire extinguisher or fire suppression system creates both a safety hazard and a food safety risk.

After any fire event or suppression system activation, operations may only resume after the affected areas have been cleaned, sanitized, inspected, and approved for use.

Guest and associate safety always come first.

---

# Purpose

Provide clear procedures for safely cleaning and restoring operations following the discharge of a fire extinguisher or automatic fire suppression system.

This policy applies to all Ordina locations.

---

# Emergency Response

After any fire or suppression system discharge:

1. Ensure all guests and associates are safe.
2. Contact emergency services if necessary.
3. Notify management immediately.
4. Follow instructions from emergency responders.
5. Do not begin cleanup until the area has been declared safe.

---

# Safety Procedures

Before beginning cleanup:

### Equipment Shutdown

- Unplug electrical equipment.
- Turn off gas appliances.
- Shut down affected cooking equipment.

### Allow Equipment to Cool

Hot surfaces must cool to room temperature before cleaning begins.

### Hot Oil Safety

Do not apply water to hot oil.

Water can cause:

- Steam burns.
- Oil splatter.
- Serious injury.

### Personal Protective Equipment

Associates performing cleanup must wear:

- Disposable gloves.
- Protective equipment as necessary.

If suppression chemicals contact skin or eyes, flush immediately with clean water and seek medical attention if necessary.

---

# Food Safety Requirements

The following items must be discarded if exposed to suppression chemicals:

- Food products.
- Cooking oil.
- Packaging materials.
- Disposable utensils.
- Single-use items.
- Exposed ingredients.

Contaminated products may never be served to guests.

---

# Cleaning Procedures

### Step 1: Remove Residue

Use warm soapy water to remove suppression chemicals from affected surfaces.

### Step 2: Rinse

Rinse surfaces thoroughly with clean water.

### Step 3: Sanitize

Apply approved sanitizer to all food contact surfaces.

### Step 4: Air Dry

Allow all equipment and surfaces to completely air dry before use.

---

# Equipment Recovery

Equipment may only be returned to service when:

- Completely cleaned.
- Fully dry.
- Properly inspected.
- Safe to operate.

If equipment damage is suspected, service technicians should inspect the equipment before use.

---

# Management Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

- Protecting guests and associates.
- Coordinating emergency response.
- Verifying food disposal.
- Supervising cleanup.
- Documenting damages.
- Arranging repairs when necessary.
- Approving reopening.

---

# Reopening Requirements

Operations may resume only when:

- The emergency has ended.
- The area has been cleaned.
- Food contact surfaces have been sanitized.
- Contaminated products have been discarded.
- Equipment is safe to operate.
- Management approves reopening.

---

# Prohibited Practices

The following actions are not permitted:

- Using contaminated food.
- Reusing contaminated oil.
- Cleaning hot oil with water.
- Operating damaged equipment.
- Reopening before sanitation is complete.
- Ignoring chemical exposure risks.

Failure to follow these procedures may result in food safety violations and corrective action.

---

# Guiding Principle

When fire suppression systems activate, safety comes first, sanitation comes second, and operations resume only when both have been restored.
