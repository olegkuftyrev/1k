# ORDINA

## Repair, Maintenance & Capital Expenditure Policy

### Summary

Repairs maintain operations. Capital expenditures improve or extend the life of company assets.

Routine repairs should be expensed as operating costs. Major repairs, equipment replacements, and long-term improvements may qualify as capital expenditures and require approval.

Proper planning and approval protect company assets and ensure responsible spending.

---

## Purpose

Provide clear guidelines for maintenance spending, capital projects, and approval requirements.

This policy applies to all Ordina locations.

---

# Repair vs. Capital Expenditure

### Repair Expense

Repairs restore equipment or facilities to working condition.

Examples include:

- Routine maintenance.
- Service calls.
- Minor repairs.
- Replacement of worn components.
- Emergency repairs.

Repair expenses are treated as operating costs.

---

### Capital Expenditure

Capital expenditures improve, replace, or significantly extend the useful life of company assets.

Projects expected to provide value for five years or longer may qualify as capital expenditures.

---

# Capital Approval Requirements

### Individual Assets

Any individual item costing **$500 or more** may qualify as a capital asset.

### Major Projects

Projects exceeding **$5,000** require a Capital Expenditure Request (ERF).

Approval must be obtained before work begins unless the repair is considered an emergency.

---

# Approval Process

Capital requests must include:

- Scope of work.
- Estimated cost.
- Business justification.
- Expected life of the investment.

Non-emergency projects require management approval before funds are committed.

---

# HVAC and Refrigeration Equipment

The following items may qualify as capital expenditures:

- Compressors.
- Condenser units.
- Condenser coils.
- Evaporator coils.
- Exhaust fan motors.
- Makeup air fan motors.
- HVAC fan assemblies.
- Major refrigeration components.

---

# Furniture and Fixtures

Examples include:

- Multiple patio umbrellas.
- Furniture replacement.
- Soda fountain counters.
- Large fixture replacements.

Furniture replacement generally qualifies when the existing equipment has reached the end of its useful life.

---

# Building Improvements

Capital projects may include:

- Ceiling tile replacement.
- Ceiling grid replacement.
- Storefront glass replacement.
- Entry door replacement.
- Exterior painting.
- Large floor re-grouting projects.
- Sign upgrades or replacements.

Preventive building maintenance protects both safety and brand appearance.

---

# Parking Lot Improvements

Examples include:

- Extensive pothole repairs.
- Seal coating.
- Surface restoration.
- Parking lot striping.

Parking areas should be maintained regularly to preserve safety and appearance.

---

# Plumbing Improvements

Capital plumbing projects may include:

- Grease trap replacement.
- Water line replacement.
- Sewer line replacement.
- Major plumbing infrastructure repairs.

---

# Electrical Improvements

Examples include:

- Electrical upgrades.
- Main breaker replacement.
- Major fixture replacement.
- Aging electrical infrastructure improvements.

---

# Store Improvement Programs

Company-wide rollout programs, equipment upgrades, and approved improvement initiatives may qualify as capital expenditures.

---

# Budget Planning

Managers should identify major repair and replacement needs during annual planning.

Proper planning helps:

- Reduce emergency expenses.
- Improve equipment reliability.
- Extend asset life.
- Protect the guest experience.

---

# Prohibited Practices

The following actions are not permitted:

- Starting major projects without approval.
- Misclassifying repair expenses.
- Delaying critical repairs that create safety risks.
- Purchasing capital equipment without authorization.
- Avoiding required approval processes.

Failure to follow approval and spending guidelines may result in corrective action.
