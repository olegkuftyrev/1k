# ORDINA

## CH-4A Manual Deposits

### Purpose

Manual deposits provide a secure method for processing payments that cannot be accepted through standard cash collection procedures.

Examples include:

- Customer checks.
- Catering payments.
- Unacceptable currency.
- Special deposits.
- Other approved payment items.

---

# Approved Uses

Manual deposits may be used for:

- Catering checks.
- Corporate account payments.
- Customer checks.
- Unaccepted bills.
- Approved non-cash payments.

Manual deposits should not be used for normal cash sales.

---

# Deposit Supplies

Locations must maintain:

- Deposit bags.
- Deposit slips.
- Deposit logs.
- Tracking documentation.

Management is responsible for ensuring adequate supplies are available.

---

# Preparing a Manual Deposit

1. Verify the payment amount.
2. Complete the deposit slip.
3. Record the total amount.
4. Place the payment and deposit slip inside the deposit bag.
5. Seal the bag completely.

Only one deposit amount should be recorded per deposit bag.

---

# Securing the Deposit

The prepared deposit shall:

- Be secured immediately.
- Be placed into the designated deposit compartment.
- Be recorded in the cash management system.
- Be documented in the deposit log.

Deposits should never remain unsecured.

---

# Collection Procedures

Authorized armored carriers or approved banking partners may collect manual deposits.

Upon collection:

- Verify the transfer.
- Obtain documentation when applicable.
- Record the collection date.
- Record tracking information.

---

# Deposit Logs

Manual deposits shall be recorded separately from regular cash deposits.

Records should include:

- Date.
- Amount.
- Deposit number.
- Description.
- Tracking information.
- Collector information.

Deposit records should be retained according to company record retention requirements.

---

# Leadership Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

- Preparing accurate deposits.
- Maintaining deposit supplies.
- Securing deposits.
- Verifying collection.
- Maintaining records.
- Investigating discrepancies.

---

# Accountability

Failure to properly secure, record, or deposit funds may result in corrective action.

Examples include:

- Missing documentation.
- Unsecured deposits.
- Delayed deposits.
- Inaccurate records.
- Failure to report discrepancies.

---

# Guiding Principle

Prepare it.

Record it.

Secure it.

Track every deposit.
