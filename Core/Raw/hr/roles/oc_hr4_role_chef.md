# HR-4 Leadership Role: Chef

## Purpose

The Chef position exists to lead Back of House (BOH) operations, develop kitchen associates, ensure food quality, and support the General Manager in delivering operational excellence.

The Chef serves as the leader of the kitchen while developing future BOH talent and maintaining exceptional food standards.

---

## Position Summary

The Chef is responsible for:

- Kitchen leadership.
- Food quality.
- Associate development.
- Inventory management.
- Food safety.
- Kitchen operations.
- Financial performance.
- Guest experience.

---

## Leadership Expectations

Chefs are expected to:

- Demonstrate workability.
- Demonstrate intensity.
- Demonstrate integrity.
- Lead by example.
- Develop people.
- Maintain standards.
- Create accountability.

Leadership begins in the kitchen.

---

## Culture Leadership

The Chef:

- Lives company values.
- Builds teamwork.
- Creates accountability.
- Encourages growth.
- Promotes ownership.
- Supports positive culture.

Kitchen culture determines operational consistency.

---

## Recruitment Responsibilities

The Chef assists in:

- Recruiting BOH associates.
- Identifying talent.
- Developing future leaders.
- Supporting succession planning.
- Building kitchen staffing.

Strong kitchens are built through strong people.

---

## Training Responsibilities

The Chef trains associates on:

- Recipes.
- Cooking procedures.
- Food safety.
- Kitchen operations.
- Company policies.
- Operational standards.

Training happens daily through coaching and example.

---

## Kitchen Operations

The Chef:

- Oversees daily kitchen execution.
- Assigns responsibilities.
- Maintains food quality.
- Ensures proper breaks.
- Controls production.
- Supports labor efficiency.

Kitchen excellence creates guest loyalty.

---

## Food Quality Leadership

The Chef ensures:

- Recipe compliance.
- Portion accuracy.
- Product freshness.
- Food presentation.
- Proper cooking techniques.
- Operational consistency.

Food quality is the foundation of the brand.

---

## Waste and Production Management

Responsibilities include:

- Controlling food waste.
- Managing batch cooking.
- Adjusting production levels.
- Supporting profitability.
- Reducing overproduction.
- Maintaining freshness.

Smart production protects both quality and profitability.

---

## Safety Responsibilities

The Chef promotes:

- Food safety.
- Workplace safety.
- Sanitation compliance.
- Safe equipment usage.
- Proper chemical handling.
- Risk reduction.

Safety is a leadership responsibility.

---

## Guest Experience

The Chef contributes to guest satisfaction through:

- Food quality.
- Speed of service.
- Product availability.
- Restaurant cleanliness.
- Hospitality support.
- Operational excellence.

Food creates emotional connections.

---

## Brand Representation

The Chef:

- Supports promotions.
- Implements new products.
- Protects recipes.
- Maintains standards.
- Supports community outreach.
- Represents company values.

---

## Financial Leadership

The Chef shares responsibility for:

- Food cost.
- Inventory accuracy.
- Waste reduction.
- Ordering.
- Labor efficiency.
- Profitability.

Every inventory decision impacts the business.

---

## Inventory and Administration

Responsibilities include:

- Inventory counts.
- Product ordering.
- Supply management.
- Administrative support.
- Equipment maintenance.
- Operational reporting.

---

## Required Qualifications

Recommended qualifications include:

- Some high school education or equivalent.
- One to two years of operations experience.
- Food safety certification.
- Flexible scheduling availability.

---

## Knowledge and Skills

Chefs should demonstrate:

- Cooking expertise.
- Leadership.
- Communication.
- Inventory management.
- Food safety knowledge.
- Financial understanding.
- Coaching ability.
- Time management.

---

## Leadership Competencies

Core competencies include:

- Developing direct reports and others.
- Listening.
- Motivating others.
- Caring about others.
- Hiring and staffing.

---

## Success Measures

Performance may be measured through:

- Food quality.
- Kitchen execution.
- Inventory accuracy.
- Food cost.
- BOH development.
- Safety performance.
- Guest satisfaction.
- Leadership effectiveness.

---

## Career Progression

Potential advancement opportunities include:

- General Manager in Training.
- General Manager.

Advancement depends on leadership ability, business results, and people development.

---

## Work Environment

Chefs frequently:

- Work in hot kitchens.
- Stand for extended periods.
- Lift products and supplies.
- Operate cooking equipment.
- Perform repetitive tasks.
- Work flexible schedules.

---

## Leadership Philosophy

The Chef succeeds by:

- Protecting food quality.
- Developing people.
- Leading the kitchen.
- Supporting the business.
- Maintaining standards.
- Preparing future leaders.

The Chef is not simply the best cook in the restaurant. The role exists to build the next generation of kitchen leaders while delivering exceptional food every day.
