# Service Animals Policy

*For General Manager, Multi-Unit Manager, and Area Leader use only.*

---

## Overview

Guests with a dog that qualifies as a Service Animal under the Americans with Disabilities Act ("ADA") must be served and allowed in all areas where guests are normally permitted.

---

## One Question to Ask a Guest with a Dog

**Ask:** *"Is your dog a service animal?"*

- **If yes** — allow the guest and their dog to stay.
- **If no** — kindly ask the guest to leave with their dog.

**Do not ask the guest:**
- To verify that the dog is a service animal
- About their disability or why a service animal is needed
- For medical documentation
- For an identification card or training documentation for the dog
- Any other questions about the dog or the guest

---

## Proper Behavior of a Service Dog

- Not required to wear a vest, ID tag, or special harness.
- **Is required to be harnessed, leashed, or tethered** (see ADA exceptions below).
- Do not permit the dog to sit on a chair, be fed at the table, or be held near the steam table.

**Say:** *"We are happy to allow your service dog to be with you while you dine with us. Seating, food, and drink are provided for guests only."*

**Do not ask the guest to leave unless the service dog is:**
- Out of control (vicious behavior or poses a direct threat to safety), or
- Not housebroken.

If removal is necessary, **do ask** the guest whether they would still like to dine without the dog.

---

## Complaints from Other Guests

If another guest reports an allergy or fear of dogs:

- **Do not** deny access or refuse service to the guest with the service dog.
- **Do** accommodate the complaining guest by relocating them to a different area of the restaurant, if possible.
- **Do not** isolate the guest with the service dog from all other guests.
- **Do** keep concerned guests a safe and appropriate distance from the service dog.

---

## ADA Note on Miniature Horses

While miniature horses are not technically "service animals" under the ADA, the ADA requires that they be permitted on the premises unless the animal:

1. Is not housebroken,
2. Is not under control,
3. Is too large for the size of the location, or
4. Poses a legitimate threat to the safety of others.
