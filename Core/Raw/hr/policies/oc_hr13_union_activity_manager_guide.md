# Union Activity — Manager Guide

**For managers only. Do not share with associates.**

This guide helps managers recognize union organizing activity, respond correctly, and escalate appropriately. Contact HR immediately with any questions.

---

## Company Position

The company respects associates' rights to express workplace concerns. We do not believe union representation is necessary. We are committed to direct conversations at the store level and solving problems together.

---

## Union Basics

### What is a union?

A labor organization, as defined by the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), represents associates in negotiations over wages and conditions of employment.

Unions are funded by dues deducted from member paychecks. In a unionized store, all associates in the bargaining unit are represented by the union — including those who voted against it.

In non-right-to-work states, all associates in a unionized store are required to pay union dues.

### How does union organizing work?

1. The union asks associates to sign **authorization cards** — legal documents indicating intent to have the union negotiate on their behalf.
2. Once 30% of associates have signed cards, the union calls for a National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) election.
3. If 50% + 1 of voting associates vote in favor, the store becomes unionized.
4. The union then negotiates a contract through **collective bargaining** — a process that can take a year or more.

### What is a strike?

An organized work stoppage by associates to pressure the employer into meeting their demands.

- Associates are not paid during a strike.
- Associates do not qualify for unemployment insurance during a strike in almost all circumstances.
- Unions can fine members who refuse to strike.
- In an economic strike, the employer may hire permanent replacements. Returning strikers may not be immediately reinstated if no positions are open.

### Right-to-work states

In right-to-work states, associates cannot currently be compelled to join a union or pay dues. However:

- All associates in the bargaining unit are represented by the union regardless of membership.
- All associates are covered by any contract the union negotiates.
- The union can prohibit non-members from voting on contracts.
- Unions may pressure non-members to join and pay dues.
- Federal law on right-to-work may change.

**Signing an authorization card is a significant legal act even in a right-to-work state.**

## Recognizing Union Activity

Signs that organizing may be underway:

- Union handouts found on premises.
- Associates signing cards or holding unusual meetings.
- Rumors of union activity or off-site union meetings.
- Unusual changes in associate behavior or unusually quiet atmosphere.
- Strangers visiting the store and speaking with associates outside of normal guest activity.
- Visits, calls, or letters from union representatives.

---

## What to Do

### If you suspect union activity

Report to HR immediately — no later than the end of your shift.

Do not threaten associates or change their schedules, discipline, or promotion opportunities because of suspected union involvement.

### If you find union handouts on premises

1. Pick up the materials.
2. Make a copy.
3. Send the copy to HR with an explanation of where and how it was found.
4. Dispose of the original.

Do this no later than the end of your shift.

### If an associate is talking about unions during work time

Associates may discuss unions the same way they discuss any other topic. You cannot treat union talk differently than other conversation.

If the conversation is pulling associates away from their work, you may ask them to save it for their break — same as you would for any discussion. Solicitation during work time is against company policy regardless of the topic.

Solicitation is permitted during meal breaks and before or after shifts.

Enforce the solicitation policy consistently and without discrimination. Report any solicitation to HR immediately.

### If an associate is distributing union literature

You may instruct the associate that materials can only be distributed:

- During non-work time for both the person distributing and the recipient.
- In non-work areas.

Distribution during work time or in work areas violates company policy. Remove any union materials posted on company bulletin boards. Report to HR immediately.

### If a non-associate is organizing on company premises

1. Notify the person in charge.
2. Politely ask the person to stop soliciting.
3. If they refuse, ask them to leave — state that soliciting is against company policy.
4. Never use physical force.
5. If they refuse to leave, contact the police.
6. Report the incident to HR immediately — no later than the end of your shift.

### If an associate wears a union button or item

Do not ask them to remove it. Instead, report the following to HR immediately:

- Associate's title.
- Whether they work front-of-house, back-of-house, or both.
- Full description of the item: size, color, message.

Wait for HR instructions before taking any action.

## Legal Do's and Don'ts

Managers are legal representatives of the company. Actions during an organizing campaign can create legal liability. Follow these rules without exception.

### You may NOT

- Ask associates whether they signed a card, support the union, or how they will vote. This is **interrogation** — an unfair labor practice.
- Take informal polls or votes.
- Attend union meetings or send an observer. This is **surveillance** — an unfair labor practice.
- Spy on union meetings or give the impression you are monitoring them.
- Distribute an anti-union petition.
- Ask job applicants about union membership, past or present.
- Threaten associates (no raise, no promotion, unfavorable schedule) to discourage union support.
- Promise or grant benefits (raise, promotion, favorable schedule) to discourage union support.

### You may

- Answer associate questions about unions, existing benefits, and company policies.
- Share factual information about unions, including:
  - Mandatory dues.
  - Risk of strikes and picket duty.
  - Reduced individual influence over scheduling and advancement.
  - Union focus on group issues, not individual concerns.
- Listen if associates voluntarily share information about union activity — without asking follow-up questions. Report what you hear to HR.

---

## Escalation

Report any union activity, materials, or incidents to HR immediately — no later than the end of your shift. When in doubt, call HR before acting.
