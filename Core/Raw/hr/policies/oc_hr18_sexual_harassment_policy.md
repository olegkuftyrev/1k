# Sexual Harassment Policy

---

## Policy Statement

All associates have the right to work in an environment free from intimidation and sexual harassment. Every person at the company is responsible for maintaining that environment.

---

## What Is Sexual Harassment?

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) defines sexual harassment as:

> **"Unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature."**

**"Unwelcome"** is the defining word. A person may appear to consent or participate in conduct and still consider it unwelcome. Whether conduct is welcome depends on all of the circumstances. Sexual conduct is unwelcome whenever the person subjected to it considers it unwelcome.

Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 gives every working person the legal right to an environment free from sex-based harassment.

---

## Two Types of Sexual Harassment

### 1. Quid Pro Quo

*Latin: "this for that."*

Quid pro quo harassment occurs when:

- Sexual conduct is made explicitly or implicitly a condition of employment, or
- Submission to or rejection of sexual advances is used as the basis for employment decisions.

**Examples:**

- An applicant is required to perform sexual favors to obtain employment.
- A supervisor demands sexual favors in return for favorable treatment: good shifts, favorable assignments, promotions, or perks.
- An associate is retaliated against for refusing a supervisor's sexual advances (demotion, worse shifts, termination).
- A qualified associate is denied benefits because another associate complied with sexual demands.

**Quid pro quo harassment can only be committed by someone in a position of authority over the victim.**

---

### 2. Hostile Environment Harassment

Conduct that unreasonably interferes with an individual's work performance or creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive working environment.

**Hostile environment harassment can be committed by supervisors, coworkers, guests, or vendors.**

#### Verbal Examples

- Referring to an adult as "girl," "hunk," "doll," "babe," or "honey."
- Whistling, cat calls, kissing sounds, howling.
- Discussing sexual activities or dating.
- Using condescending terms: "kiddo," "missy."
- Telling sexual jokes or stories.
- Asking about sexual fantasies, preferences, or history.
- Making sexual comments about a person's clothing, anatomy, or appearance.
- Repeatedly asking out a person who is not interested.
- Spreading rumors about a person's sex life.
- Unwanted phone calls.
- Using profane or vulgar language.

#### Non-Verbal Examples

- Looking a person up and down (elevator eyes).
- Staring or winking in a way that implies a sexual message.
- Blocking a person's path.
- Following a person.
- Giving unsolicited personal gifts or letters.
- Displaying sexually suggestive visuals: posters, cartoons, calendars, drawings.
- Making sexual gestures with hands or body movements.

#### Physical Examples

- Massaging a person's neck or shoulders.
- Touching a person's clothing, hair, or body.
- Hugging, kissing, patting, stroking, tickling, or pinching.
- Touching or rubbing oneself sexually around another person.
- Standing too close or brushing up against a person.
- Blocking normal movement.
- Actual or attempted rape or sexual assault.

## What to Do If You Are Harassed

1. **Report it immediately** to your manager or HR.
2. Stay calm and professional.
3. Be direct — tell the offender and your manager right away.
4. When reporting, provide all relevant facts: who, what, when, where, and how.

---

## How HR Handles a Sexual Harassment Complaint

**Notify HR whenever you receive a complaint. HR will direct you on proper procedures.**

### Receiving the Complaint

How you initially receive a complaint determines whether the associate trusts you to correct the situation.

Take every complaint seriously. There are no stereotypical victims or harassers.

When an associate brings a complaint:

- Acknowledge that you appreciate them coming forward.
- Affirm their right to a harassment-free workplace.
- Acknowledge that this is difficult to discuss and that you want to resolve it.
- Immediately assure them of: confidentiality, a safe environment, and no retaliation.
- Explain that a thorough investigation will be conducted immediately by HR.

Ask the associate to be specific. You cannot investigate or stop behavior you don't understand in detail. Take notes throughout. Key questions to ask (use judgment on which apply):

- Who is the harasser?
- What time and where did it happen?
- What were you doing? What was the harasser doing?
- What was said or shown? Where were you touched?
- How did you respond? Did you tell them to stop?
- Did you tell anyone else? Were there witnesses?
- Has this happened before?

Review your notes. Write everything down.

---

### Interviewing the Alleged Harasser

1. You must interview the alleged harasser.
2. Keep the conversation confidential.
3. Do not initially reveal the complainant's identity. Describe the circumstances instead: *"Did you touch the back of a coworker near the service station around 10:00 a.m. this morning?"*
4. Be direct: *"You have been accused of inappropriate behavior. It may not have been your intention, but the impact of your behavior may constitute sexual harassment. Sexual harassment is against the law and violates company policy."*
5. Stay neutral and on topic.
6. If the person admits the behavior, tell them it must stop and that action is forthcoming.
7. If they deny, explain that you are gathering facts from all sides before making a determination.
8. Emphasize: everyone has the right to work free from sexual harassment.
9. Emphasize: retaliation will not be tolerated.
10. Document everything. Write it all down.

---

### Interviewing Witnesses

1. Interview all witnesses.
2. Do not initially identify the complainant or the alleged harasser. Say: *"Your name was given to me as someone who may have observed an interaction between associates. I'd like to talk about your observations."*
3. Describe the situation and ask witnesses to share what they saw. Focus on observations — not assumptions, hearsay, or opinions about the people involved.
4. Conduct all conversations in private.

---

### Follow-Up with the Alleged Harasser

- Gather thorough and complete statements during the investigation.
- When the investigation is complete, take appropriate disciplinary action.
- Keep investigation records separate from the associate's personnel file. If the harasser repeats the behavior, prior records may be used to support termination. If they reform, the investigation remains confidential.

---

### Follow-Up with the Complainant

1. Notify the complainant that the incident was investigated and action was taken.
2. Ask them to notify you if it happens again.
3. Thank them for coming forward.

## Management Responsibilities

The company is responsible for the acts of its supervisors with respect to sexual harassment — regardless of whether the conduct was authorized or forbidden, and regardless of whether the company knew it occurred.

Managers must:

**1. Understand**
- Know this policy and ensure it is posted where all associates can see it.
- Consider the impact, not just the intent, of words and actions.
- Do not assume associates enjoy sexually oriented behavior.
- Do not assume associates will speak up when offended.

**2. Observe**
- Watch and listen for warning signs.
- Pay attention to how associates interact.
- Be sensitive to how more vulnerable associates may react to others' behavior.
- Watch for subtle forms of harassment.

**3. Be a Role Model**
- Do not encourage harassers by laughing at their jokes or participating. This signals that the behavior is acceptable.

**4. Zero Tolerance**
- Exhibit the behavior you expect from associates.

**5. Non-Fraternization**
Romantic relationships between associates are discouraged when:
- One associate is in a supervisory position over the other.
- Both are supervisors and one supervises the other.
- Both are in the same department and one holds a higher-level position.

The company does not seek to interfere with associates' personal lives, but may consider a supervisory associate who enters such a relationship to have exercised poor judgment. This may result in a change in supervisory status or other disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

**6. Open Door Policy**
Demonstrate willingness to discuss concerns at any time.

**7. Take Action**
- Communicate that you will take immediate action on any complaint.
- Managers, Area Leaders, and Regional Leaders who receive a harassment complaint — whether formal or informal, written or verbal — must report it to HR within **72 hours**.

**8. No Retaliation**
Do not retaliate against anyone who files a sexual harassment complaint. Retaliation includes remarks, schedule changes, or creating an environment that discourages future complaints.

**9. Train Associates**
Ensure all associates know how to file a sexual harassment complaint and who to contact.
