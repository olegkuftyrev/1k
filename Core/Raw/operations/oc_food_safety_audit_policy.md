# ORDINA

## Food Safety Audit Policy

### Summary

Food safety audits protect our guests, associates, and the reputation of the brand.

Routine, random, and revisit audits measure operational performance and compliance. Audit results affect accountability, recognition, and performance standards.

Every location is expected to maintain food safety standards every day, not only during scheduled inspections.

---

## Purpose

Establish clear expectations for food safety audits and audit accountability.

This policy applies to all Ordina locations.

---

# Audit Types

Ordina uses three types of food safety audits:

- Routine Audits
- Random Audits
- Revisit Audits

---

# Routine Audits

Routine audits are scheduled evaluations of food safety and operational standards.

These audits measure:

- Food safety compliance.
- Sanitation.
- Facility condition.
- Operational execution.
- Associate knowledge.

Routine audits are used as the primary operational assessment.

---

# Random Audits

Random audits are conducted without notice.

Their purpose is to verify that standards are being maintained every day.

### Random Audit Standards

- Random audits may occur at any location.
- Locations should always operate at audit-ready standards.
- Random audits supersede routine audits for performance measurement purposes.

For scorecard purposes, the average of two audit scores may be used.

If a location fails a routine audit but passes the random audit, the location may remain eligible for performance incentives.

---

# Revisit Audits

Revisit audits occur after an unsuccessful audit to verify corrective actions.

A revisit confirms whether deficiencies have been corrected.

### Important

Passing a revisit does not replace a failed audit.

If a location fails a routine audit and later passes the revisit, the original failed audit remains part of the performance record.

Locations may still be ineligible for incentive programs based on the original result.

---

# Daily Expectations

Every location is expected to maintain:

- Safe food handling practices.
- Clean facilities.
- Proper sanitation.
- Accurate temperature controls.
- Equipment maintenance.
- Strong operational execution.

Audit readiness is an everyday responsibility.

---

# Management Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

- Preparing associates.
- Maintaining standards.
- Correcting deficiencies.
- Conducting internal inspections.
- Following up on action plans.
- Supporting continuous improvement.

---

# Corrective Actions

When deficiencies are identified:

1. Correct the issue immediately.
2. Identify the root cause.
3. Train associates if necessary.
4. Document corrective actions.
5. Verify long-term compliance.

---

# Audit Philosophy

Audits are not intended to punish teams.

They exist to:

- Protect guests.
- Protect associates.
- Improve operations.
- Maintain brand standards.
- Promote accountability.

Success is achieved by maintaining standards every day, not by preparing only for inspections.

---

# Prohibited Practices

The following actions are not permitted:

- Falsifying records.
- Concealing violations.
- Delaying corrective actions.
- Preparing only for scheduled audits.
- Ignoring food safety concerns.
- Altering documentation.

Failure to maintain food safety standards may result in corrective action and operational review.
