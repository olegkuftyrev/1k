# Outside Agency Inspections

---

## OSHA — Occupational Safety and Health Administration

OSHA enforces workplace safety under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (OSH Act, 1970). Inspections are conducted by OSHA compliance officers and may result in citations, correction deadlines, and penalties.

A standard inspection has three parts: opening conference, workplace tour, closing conference. Each step is an opportunity to demonstrate the safety measures the company has taken.

### When an OSHA Inspector Arrives

**Step 1 — Call your Area Leader immediately.**

**Step 2 — Verify credentials.**
Ask to see the compliance officer's identification before the inspection begins. In most cases the officer will present credentials without being asked. If not, request and review them before allowing the inspection to proceed.

**Step 3 — Determine the reason for the inspection.**
Ask why the inspection is being conducted before it begins.

- **Routine inspection:** no associate complaint was filed.
- **Complaint-based inspection:** ask for a copy of the complaint. If the associate requested anonymity, their name will be removed from the copy.

Do not take any adverse action against an associate who filed a complaint. This is prohibited.

### Opening Conference

The compliance officer may ask to review company records. OSHA regulations require that these records be made available. Contact HR if you need assistance.

### Workplace Tour

**Interviews:** Permit the compliance officer to interview non-supervisory associates only when necessary to carry out the inspection. Do not allow interviews to pull associates away from their workstations for extended periods.

**Note taking:** Document throughout the tour:
- Areas of the restaurant visited.
- Names of all individuals involved in any way.
- Favorable and unfavorable comments made by the compliance officer.
- Any comments made by associates.

**Photos and samples:** Compliance officers may take photos and environmental samples. If possible, take the same photos the officer takes. If no camera is available, write a detailed description of each photo taken.

**Answering questions:** Answer only the specific questions asked. Do not volunteer additional information. Do not make unnecessary admissions or statements that could be misconstrued.

### Closing Conference

The compliance officer will meet with the manager after the tour and may present findings.

- Do not negotiate the terms of any potential citation with the compliance officer.
- Do not make commitments or promises about corrective action or timelines.
- If the officer asks for a commitment on correction, explain that until a citation has been officially issued, you are not in a position to discuss abatement of alleged violations.
- Do not ask the compliance officer to give a general opinion on the restaurant's safety condition.
- You may ask for clarification on specific points raised during the inspection.

### After the Inspection — Citation

If a citation is issued:

1. Forward a copy to Risk Management immediately.
2. Post the citation in or near the area cited, or in a visible location where all associates will see it (e.g., near the time clock).

Risk Management will advise on the correction process.

## Health Department

The Health Department enforces health and safety codes for food service operations. Inspections may be routine or triggered by a guest complaint.

### When a Health Inspector Arrives

1. Accompany the inspector at all times during the inspection.
2. Keep communication open. Ask for advice or recommendations on correcting any violations identified. Never confront or argue with the inspector.
3. After the inspection, send a copy of the inspection report to Risk Management and notify your Area Leader. Discuss the inspection findings and any violations.
4. Post the original inspection report in a visible location in the kitchen.
5. Correct all violations immediately. Communicate the corrective procedures to all associates and confirm they are following the updated procedures.

## Department of Labor

The Department of Labor enforces federal and state wage and hour laws.

An inspector may visit your location and may request valid work permits for minor associates.

Know the federal and state labor laws that apply to your location. Refer to posted state and federal labor law notices or contact HR with questions.

## Unemployment Claims

The company uses a third-party unemployment claims representative to manage unemployment filings on the company's behalf.

### When an Associate Files for Unemployment

1. Fax or submit the unemployment claim to HR immediately, along with an explanation of why the associate was terminated or resigned.
2. HR will complete the required forms and submit them to the unemployment representative.
3. The state Unemployment Department will issue a ruling based on information provided by both the associate and the company.
4. If either party appeals, the Unemployment Department will schedule a hearing.

Managers are expected to attend hearings when possible — they are most familiar with the facts. HR is available to support. Additional assistance is available for managers in remote locations.

### Why Timeliness Matters

Notify HR of all terminations and resignations promptly. Late responses to unemployment claims create avoidable losses.

Each location contributes a percentage of associate wages into the state unemployment pool (typically 0.3%–0.7%). Following correct disciplinary procedures and minimizing preventable claims helps keep that contribution rate low.

The best way to reduce unemployment claims is to document performance issues consistently and follow progressive discipline procedures.
