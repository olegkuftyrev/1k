# ORDINA

## CH-4 Safe & Register Funds

### Summary

Safe and register funds exist to support daily business operations and provide associates with the necessary change to serve guests efficiently.

All funds must remain secure, accurately accounted for, and protected from loss, theft, or misuse.

---

# Purpose

This policy establishes standards for:

- Change funds.
- Register funds.
- Safe accountability.
- Daily cash counts.
- Change fund adjustments.
- Cash storage requirements.

---

# Authorized Fund Levels

Each location shall maintain authorized:

- Register starting funds.
- Change funds.
- Safe balances.

Leadership shall establish and document authorized fund amounts for each location.

Authorized balances may vary based on:

- Sales volume.
- Business hours.
- Drive-thru operations.
- Seasonal business levels.
- Operational needs.

---

# Change Fund Replenishment

Change orders should be kept as small as practical while maintaining operational needs.

Change funds should be replenished through approved banking or armored carrier services.

Payment for change orders should occur promptly according to established procedures.

Locations should:

- Prepare change order payments before delivery.
- Maintain proper documentation.
- Record change order activity.
- Verify all deliveries.

---

# Change Order Verification

Upon receiving a change order:

1. Verify the amount received.
2. Count the funds immediately.
3. Secure the funds in the safe.
4. Report discrepancies immediately.
5. Retain supporting documentation.

Any shortages, overages, or delivery issues must be investigated promptly.

---

# Change Fund Increases

Permanent increases to authorized change funds require leadership approval.

Requests should consider:

- Sales growth.
- Increased transaction volume.
- Extended business hours.
- Seasonal business needs.

Temporary increases may be approved during:

- Holidays.
- Promotional events.
- Special events.
- Unusual business volume.

---

# Change Fund Reductions

If a location consistently maintains excess cash beyond operational requirements, authorized balances should be reviewed.

Excess funds should be:

- Deposited separately.
- Properly documented.
- Recorded accurately.

---

# Daily Cash Counts

All safe and register funds must be counted daily.

Counts should occur:

### Start of Day

- Safe funds verified.
- Register funds verified.
- Shortages or overages investigated.

### End of Day

- All cash counted.
- Register funds verified.
- Safe balances confirmed.
- Deposits completed.
- Discrepancies investigated.

---

# Safe Accountability

Safe funds and register funds should always remain intact.

Cash shortages, overages, or adjustments should never be used to offset other discrepancies.

Example:

If a location is assigned:

- $1,500 in change and register funds

Then the safe and registers combined should contain:

- $1,500 at the end of the day.

---

# Cash Shortages and Overages

Any discrepancy must be:

- Investigated immediately.
- Documented.
- Reported to leadership.
- Resolved whenever possible.

Cash overages may not be:

- Held to offset future shortages.
- Used for purchases.
- Used for employee events.
- Used for tips.
- Removed from the business.

Cash shortages may never be covered by:

- Associate personal funds.
- Management personal funds.
- Donations.
- Unauthorized cash sources.

---

# Overnight Cash Storage

At the end of each business day:

- All register funds must be secured.
- All change funds must be secured.
- Deposits must be secured.
- Safe doors must be locked.

Only the authorized operating funds and current deposits should remain in the location overnight.

---

# Leadership Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

- Daily verification of funds.
- Investigating discrepancies.
- Maintaining authorized balances.
- Securing cash.
- Ensuring accurate documentation.
- Training associates on cash procedures.

---

# Accountability

Failure to comply with this policy may result in corrective action, up to and including termination.

Examples include:

- Inaccurate counts.
- Failure to secure cash.
- Unauthorized adjustments.
- Falsification of counts.
- Improper use of funds.
- Failure to report discrepancies.

---

# Guiding Principle

Count it.

Verify it.

Secure it.

Every dollar must be accounted for.
