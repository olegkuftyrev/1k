# ORDINA

## Burglary Prevention Policy

### Summary

Burglary prevention begins before a crime occurs.

Most burglaries target businesses that appear vulnerable, poorly secured, or predictable. Strong security practices, proper maintenance, and consistent procedures help protect our associates, facilities, and assets.

Security is everyone's responsibility.

---

# Purpose

Establish security standards that reduce the risk of burglary, unauthorized entry, and property loss.

This policy applies to all Ordina locations.

---

# Understanding Burglary Risk

Burglary is the unlawful entry into a closed or unoccupied business for the purpose of theft, vandalism, or other criminal activity.

Most burglaries:

- Occur when the restaurant is empty.
- Happen during overnight hours.
- Are planned in advance.
- Target locations with weak security.
- Exploit predictable routines or vulnerabilities.

Prevention depends on reducing opportunity.

---

# Layered Security Approach

Ordina uses multiple layers of protection to discourage criminal activity.

No single security measure is sufficient on its own.

---

# Perimeter Security

Exterior barriers help prevent unauthorized access.

Managers should ensure:

- Gates remain secured.
- Fences are maintained.
- Access points remain controlled.
- Exterior doors remain closed and locked.

---

# Video Surveillance

CCTV systems must:

- Operate continuously.
- Cover entrances and exits.
- Monitor vulnerable areas.
- Record properly.

Camera systems should be inspected regularly.

---

# Exterior Lighting

Adequate lighting helps:

- Deter criminal activity.
- Improve visibility.
- Support camera performance.
- Increase associate safety.

Exterior lighting should remain operational at all times.

---

# Lock and Key Security

Keys, codes, and access credentials must be protected.

Managers must ensure:

- Keys are not shared.
- Alarm codes remain confidential.
- Access codes are removed immediately after separation or transfer.
- Doors and windows are verified secure at closing.

Most security breaches involve known individuals.

---

# Alarm Systems

Alarm systems must be activated whenever the building is unoccupied.

Security systems should include:

- Door monitoring.
- Motion detection.
- Intrusion alarms.
- Emergency notification systems.

Any malfunction must be reported immediately.

---

# Landscape Management

Exterior landscaping should not create hiding places.

Managers should:

- Maintain shrubs and vegetation.
- Keep entrances visible.
- Trim trees and overgrowth.
- Maintain clear sight lines around the building.

Well-maintained property improves both safety and visibility.

---

# Interior Lighting

Limited interior lighting may remain on after closing to:

- Improve camera visibility.
- Increase deterrence.
- Allow monitoring of interior areas.

Lighting levels should support security systems.

---

# Closing Security Procedures

At closing, management must verify:

☑ Exterior doors are locked.

☑ Drive-thru windows are secured.

☑ Alarm systems are activated.

☑ Safe is secured.

☑ Cash is properly stored.

☑ Cameras are functioning.

☑ No unauthorized persons remain in the building.

---

# Management Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

- Maintaining security systems.
- Protecting company assets.
- Verifying building security.
- Reporting suspicious activity.
- Monitoring vulnerabilities.
- Training associates on security procedures.

---

# Prohibited Practices

The following actions are not permitted:

- Sharing alarm codes.
- Sharing keys.
- Leaving doors unlocked.
- Disabling security equipment.
- Leaving cash unsecured.
- Ignoring damaged locks or cameras.
- Allowing unauthorized access.

Violations of this policy may result in corrective action.

---

# Guiding Principle

A secure restaurant is a protected restaurant.

Strong security measures discourage criminal activity before it occurs.
