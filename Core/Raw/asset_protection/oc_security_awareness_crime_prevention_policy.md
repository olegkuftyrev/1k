# ORDINA

## Security Awareness & Crime Prevention Policy

### Summary

Criminal activity often occurs when opportunity, access, and low risk are present.

Ordina locations should maintain strong security practices that reduce opportunity, increase visibility, and discourage criminal activity.

Effective security is built through preparation, awareness, and consistent execution.

---

# Purpose

Establish security principles that reduce the risk of robbery, burglary, theft, and other criminal activity.

This policy applies to all Ordina locations.

---

# Security Risk Factors

Criminals often evaluate three factors when selecting a target:

1. Opportunity.
2. Accessibility.
3. Risk of identification.

Reducing these factors decreases the likelihood of criminal activity.

---

# Cash Exposure

Large amounts of visible cash increase risk.

Managers should:

- Conduct regular cash pulls.
- Minimize register balances.
- Secure deposits promptly.
- Limit unnecessary cash exposure.

Cash should remain in secured storage whenever possible.

---

# Access Control

Easy access and quick escape routes may increase vulnerability.

Managers should:

- Verify doors are secured.
- Control secondary entrances.
- Monitor emergency exits.
- Limit unnecessary access points during late hours.

Security decisions must always comply with local fire and life-safety requirements.

---

# Visibility and Identification

Criminal activity is less likely when individuals believe they can be identified.

Locations should maintain:

- Adequate exterior lighting.
- Properly functioning CCTV systems.
- Clear visibility around entrances.
- Unobstructed camera views.

Well-lit environments improve both safety and deterrence.

---

# Layered Security

Security measures work best when multiple systems operate together.

Examples include:

- Fencing or perimeter barriers.
- Exterior lighting.
- Security cameras.
- Alarm systems.
- Controlled access.
- Cash controls.
- Operational awareness.

No single security measure is sufficient on its own.

---

# Property Maintenance

Managers should maintain:

- Clear landscaping.
- Unobstructed sight lines.
- Clean exterior areas.
- Visible entrances and exits.

Overgrown vegetation and hidden areas may increase security risks.

---

# Security Assessments

Leadership should periodically review:

- Lighting conditions.
- Camera coverage.
- Door security.
- Cash handling practices.
- Exterior vulnerabilities.
- Crime trends in the surrounding area.

Corrective actions should be implemented when risks are identified.

---

# Management Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

- Maintaining security systems.
- Protecting associates and guests.
- Monitoring vulnerabilities.
- Reporting concerns.
- Promoting security awareness.

---

# Guiding Principle

Security is not created by a single lock, camera, or alarm.

Security is created through awareness, preparation, and consistent execution.
