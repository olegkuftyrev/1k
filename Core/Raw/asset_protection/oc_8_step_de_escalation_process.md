# ORDINA

## 8-Step De-escalation Process

### Summary

The purpose of de-escalation is to reduce tension, protect people, and resolve conflict safely.

Associates and managers should use these steps whenever dealing with upset guests, emotional associates, conflicts, or potentially disruptive situations.

Personal safety always takes priority.

---

# Step 1: Introduce Yourself

Approach calmly and professionally.

- Identify yourself.
- Introduce your role.
- Ask for the person's name if appropriate.
- Speak respectfully.

Example:

> "Hi, my name is Alex, and I'm the manager on duty. How can I help you today?"

---

# Step 2: Listen First

Allow the individual to explain the situation.

- Listen without interrupting.
- Maintain appropriate eye contact.
- Stay calm.
- Focus on understanding.

People often become calmer when they feel heard.

---

# Step 3: Acknowledge and Clarify

Repeat the concern in your own words.

Examples:

> "Let me make sure I understand."

> "It sounds like you're frustrated because of what happened."

> "I can see why that would be upsetting."

Validation does not mean agreement.

It means understanding.

---

# Step 4: Explain the Situation

Clearly explain:

- What happened.
- Company policy.
- Available options.
- Next steps.

Use facts and avoid arguments.

---

# Step 5: Present Solutions

Whenever possible, offer reasonable options.

Examples:

- Alternative seating.
- Replacement products.
- Refund procedures.
- Manager assistance.
- Additional support.

People cooperate more readily when they have choices.

---

# Step 6: Explain Consequences

If behavior continues, calmly explain the consequences.

Examples:

> "If the behavior continues, I may need to ask you to leave."

> "If we cannot resolve this calmly, I may need to involve management or security."

Remain professional.

Never threaten.

---

# Step 7: Make a Final Request

Provide one clear and reasonable request.

Examples:

> "I'm asking you to lower your voice so we can continue helping you."

> "I'm asking you to step outside with me so we can talk."

Honor commitments that you make.

Trust is important.

---

# Step 8: Escalate When Necessary

If the situation cannot be safely resolved:

- Contact another manager.
- Request security assistance.
- Contact law enforcement.
- Remove associates and guests from danger.

Personal safety always comes first.

---

# De-escalation Principles

✔ Stay calm.

✔ Speak slowly.

✔ Listen actively.

✔ Maintain respectful language.

✔ Give space.

✔ Avoid arguing.

✔ Avoid threats.

✔ Protect people.

---

# When Not to De-escalate

Do not continue engagement if:

- A weapon is present.
- Violence occurs.
- Threats become immediate.
- The individual becomes physically aggressive.
- Safety is at risk.

Immediately seek assistance.

---

# Management Responsibilities

Managers are expected to:

- Lead calmly.
- Protect associates.
- Support guests.
- Make safety decisions.
- Escalate appropriately.

---

# Guiding Principle

Calm people.

Protect people.

Resolve the situation whenever possible.

When safety becomes uncertain, seek help immediately.
