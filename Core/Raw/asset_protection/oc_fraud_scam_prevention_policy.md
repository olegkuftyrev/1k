# ORDINA

## Fraud & Scam Prevention Policy

### Summary

Scammers often target restaurants by creating urgency, fear, or confusion.

Fraud attempts may occur by phone, email, text message, invoices, social media, or in person. Associates should never feel pressured to make immediate decisions involving money, personal information, or company assets.

When in doubt, stop, verify, and escalate.

---

# Purpose

Establish procedures to protect associates, guests, and company assets from fraud, scams, impersonation, and social engineering attempts.

This policy applies to all Ordina associates and leaders.

---

# General Rules

Associates must:

- Remain calm.
- Verify unusual requests.
- Report suspicious activity.
- Protect company information.
- Protect personal information.

Pressure and urgency are common fraud tactics.

---

# Payment and Collection Scams

Fraudsters may claim to represent:

- Utility companies.
- Vendors.
- Delivery services.
- Government agencies.
- Financial institutions.
- Company leadership.

They may threaten:

- Service interruptions.
- Fines.
- Penalties.
- Account closures.
- Immediate action.

Associates should never make payments based on verbal instructions or threats.

---

# Payment Rules

Associates may never:

- Use personal funds.
- Use store funds without authorization.
- Purchase gift cards.
- Transfer money electronically.
- Deposit company funds into cryptocurrency accounts.
- Send payments based on phone instructions.

All payment concerns should be directed to management.

---

# Personal Information Protection

Criminals may attempt to obtain:

- Social Security numbers.
- Bank account information.
- Credit card information.
- Payroll information.
- Personal identification information.
- Company credentials.

Associates should never provide sensitive information to unknown individuals.

---

# Government Impersonation Scams

Fraudsters may falsely claim to represent:

- Law enforcement.
- Government agencies.
- Tax authorities.
- Regulatory agencies.

Threats involving arrest, fines, or legal action are common scam techniques.

If such contact occurs:

- End the conversation.
- Notify management.
- Verify through official channels.

---

# Directory and Billing Scams

Fraudsters may attempt to:

- Update company information.
- Verify listings.
- Confirm advertisements.
- Request verbal approvals.

Unauthorized agreements may result in false invoices.

Associates should not authorize services without management approval.

---

# Corporate Impersonation Scams

Criminals may falsely claim to represent:

- Company leadership.
- Support teams.
- Technology departments.
- Operations leadership.

They may possess:

- Names of managers.
- Store information.
- Employee information.
- Operational knowledge.

This information does not verify legitimacy.

---

# Verification Procedures

If a suspicious request occurs:

1. Remain calm.
2. Do not provide information.
3. Request a callback number.
4. End the conversation.
5. Contact management.
6. Verify through approved company contacts.

---

# Red Flags

Warning signs include:

- Immediate payment demands.
- Threats or intimidation.
- Requests for secrecy.
- Requests for cash.
- Requests to remove money from the store.
- Requests for personal information.
- Pressure to act immediately.
- Unusual after-hours calls.

---

# Management Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

- Educating associates.
- Investigating suspicious requests.
- Reporting fraud attempts.
- Protecting company assets.
- Verifying unusual instructions.

---

# Prohibited Actions

Associates may not:

- Remove cash from the store without authorization.
- Share company information.
- Share personal information.
- Process payments without approval.
- Follow suspicious instructions.
- Ignore suspected scams.

Violations of this policy may result in corrective action.

---

# Guiding Principle

Stop.

Verify.

Protect.

If something feels unusual, confirm it before taking action.
