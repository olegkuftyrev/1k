# ORDINA

## Layers of Security Policy

### Summary

The most effective way to prevent burglary, robbery, theft, and unauthorized access is through multiple layers of security.

No single lock, camera, alarm, or procedure can fully protect a business. Security is achieved when multiple systems work together to reduce opportunity, increase visibility, and discourage criminal activity.

Every layer strengthens the next.

---

# Purpose

Establish the security principles used to protect associates, guests, assets, and company property.

This policy applies to all Ordina locations.

---

# Security Philosophy

Criminals generally seek:

- Easy access.
- Low risk.
- Limited visibility.
- Quick escape.

The purpose of layered security is to make criminal activity difficult, highly visible, and high risk.

---

# Layer 1: Property Appearance

A clean and maintained exterior serves as the first line of defense.

Management should ensure:

- Parking areas remain clean.
- Trash is removed regularly.
- Exterior areas are maintained.
- Landscaping is controlled.
- Sight lines remain clear.

Well-maintained properties discourage criminal activity.

---

# Layer 2: Landscaping

Landscaping should improve visibility.

Recommended standards:

- Tree branches maintained above pedestrian sight lines.
- Shrubs kept low enough to maintain visibility.
- Entrances and windows remain unobstructed.
- No concealed hiding areas near the building.

Landscaping should support safety.

---

# Layer 3: Lighting

Proper lighting increases visibility and deters crime.

Management should:

- Inspect exterior lighting regularly.
- Replace failed fixtures promptly.
- Ensure parking areas remain illuminated.
- Maintain lighting around entrances and exits.

Interior security lighting should remain operational after closing when appropriate.

---

# Layer 4: Video Surveillance

Camera systems provide deterrence, documentation, and accountability.

Managers should:

- Verify cameras are operational daily.
- Ensure recording systems function properly.
- Review footage when necessary.
- Maintain clear camera views.

Video systems support both security and operational excellence.

---

# Layer 5: Access Control

Controlling access reduces security risks.

Managers should:

- Keep doors secured.
- Control secondary entrances.
- Monitor key distribution.
- Remove access when employment ends.

Access should only be provided to authorized individuals.

---

# Layer 6: Keys and Credentials

Keys, alarm codes, and access credentials must be protected.

Requirements:

- No code sharing.
- No key sharing.
- Immediate removal of inactive users.
- Secure storage of keys.

Every user should have individual accountability.

---

# Layer 7: Alarm Systems

Intrusion detection systems provide additional protection.

Management should:

- Maintain alarm systems.
- Test systems periodically.
- Ensure users are trained.
- Report malfunctions immediately.

False alarms should be minimized through proper training.

---

# Layer 8: Safe Security

The safe protects company funds and sensitive assets.

Managers should:

- Keep the safe locked.
- Maintain time delays when available.
- Use individual access codes.
- Store deposits securely.
- Limit non-delay access.

Cash exposure should always be minimized.

---

# Layer 9: Cash Controls

Cash handling procedures reduce theft opportunities.

This includes:

- Regular cash pulls.
- Secure deposits.
- Register accountability.
- Daily safe counts.
- Limited cash exposure.

Cash controls protect both associates and the business.

---

# Security Awareness

Associates should remain aware of:

- Suspicious activity.
- Unusual behavior.
- Security concerns.
- Unsafe conditions.
- Operational vulnerabilities.

Early reporting often prevents incidents.

---

# Management Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

- Maintaining security systems.
- Training associates.
- Conducting inspections.
- Correcting vulnerabilities.
- Protecting associates and guests.

Security begins with leadership.

---

# Guiding Principle

One lock can fail.

One camera can fail.

One alarm can fail.

Multiple layers working together create a safer workplace.

Security is not one system.

Security is the combination of many systems working together.
